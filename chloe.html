14 May 2026

Why I Chose Multimedia Design

I almost went a completely different route before landing here — and it turned out to be exactly the right shift.

Read entry

I came into university expecting to study something far more technical, and design wasn't originally on the radar. But the more I worked on visual projects for fun — posters for friends' events, little logo ideas, doodles that turned into something more — the clearer it became that I wanted to spend my degree here instead. Multimedia Design let me keep the problem-solving mindset I liked, just pointed at typography, colour, and layout instead of code.

A year in, I don't regret the switch once. Every brief is a small puzzle: who is this for, what does it need to say, and how do I say it with as little noise as possible. That's the part I look forward to most.

2 June 2026

Inside My Toucan Books Logo Process

Picking a fictional brand was the fun part. Making its mark actually work at every size was the real assignment.

Read entry

When the brief said "invent a brand and design its logo," I knew I wanted something playful with a bit of personality behind it — so I invented Toucan Books, a fictional publishing house. The toucan's beak gave me a natural, distinctive shape to build the wordmark around, so I sketched the bird first and let the lettering grow out of it rather than treating them as two separate pieces.

The hardest part wasn't the concept, it was restraint: cutting detail until the mark still read clearly shrunk down to a tote bag or a spine label. Testing it across the mockups — packaging, a canvas bag, a promotional sleeve — showed me exactly where the design held up and where it needed simplifying.

19 June 2026

Telling One Story, Three Posters

Same event, same date, three completely different feelings — depending on whether type or image leads.

Read entry

For the "Liefde by die Dam" poster series I set myself a constraint: design three posters for the same fictional event, but let a different element lead each time — one typography-dominant, one typography-only, one image-dominant. It sounds simple until you actually try to keep the tone consistent while the visual weight shifts completely.

What surprised me most was how much the "typography-only" version had to work to still feel warm and inviting without a single illustration to lean on — colour and letterform personality had to do all of the emotional heavy lifting.

6 July 2026

First Impressions: A Year with Procreate

It started as the app I used for quick sketches. It ended up shaping how I illustrate everything else.

Read entry

I picked up Procreate mostly to rough out ideas before moving them into Illustrator, and didn't expect it to become part of my actual finished work. The Fantastic Mr Fox cover redesign is the clearest example — the looseness of brush illustration gave the characters warmth that a purely vector approach wouldn't have.

A year in, my workflow is usually: sketch and illustrate in Procreate, then bring things into Illustrator or Photoshop for typesetting and final composition. Two very different tools that, together, cover a lot more ground than either one alone.
